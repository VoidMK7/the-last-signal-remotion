# THE LAST SIGNAL: THE PRICE OF TOMORROW

**Format:** Cinematic 3D animated short | **Target runtime:** 10m 48s | **Setting:** Lagos, Nigeria and the digital world

## Logline

Malik Adeyemi carries the weight of his struggling family while trying to build a future for himself. After a painful breakup, he commits to learning, working and rebuilding after setbacks. Months later, an extraordinary and fictional memecoin windfall changes his family's circumstances—but the real victory is discovering that his worth was never measured by money.

## Main cast

- **Malik Adeyemi (24):** Thoughtful, hardworking, initially driven by shame and urgency; grows into a patient, grounded young man.
- **Mama Adeyemi:** A hardworking food seller whose quiet care keeps the family together.
- **Pa Adeyemi:** A mechanic whose workshop and health have been strained by years of work.
- **Nia:** Malik's girlfriend at the start. She leaves for a wealthier lifestyle; her choice hurts him, but she is not portrayed as a cartoon villain.
- **Tunde:** Malik's practical friend, who encourages learning and warns against hype.

## Scene 1 — The Weight of Home (0:00–1:18)

**Dawn, Lagos.** An aerial drift over a waking city descends to a modest family home. Mama counts a few notes beside her food tray. Pa repairs a generator. Malik quietly leaves his transport money on the table.

**MAMA:** “You kept this for yourself?”  
**MALIK:** “I can walk to work. Use it for the food.”  
**PA:** “You cannot carry the whole house on your back, my son.”  
**MALIK:** “I know, Papa. Just let me help today.”

Narration: “Some people are born with a head start. Malik was born with a list of things that needed fixing.”

## Scene 2 — The Life He Couldn't Afford (1:18–2:30)

Outside a bright café, Malik meets Nia. A polished car arrives; a wealthy man greets her. Nia tells Malik she is tired of waiting for a future that never seems to arrive. She leaves with the man. Malik is hurt, but the story makes clear that her decision does not define his worth.

**MALIK:** “I'm working. I'm trying to build something.”  
**NIA:** “I know. But trying doesn't change what life feels like right now.”

At home, Malik turns his phone face down, opens a notebook and writes: “What can I change tomorrow?”

Narration: “For a while, he mistook someone else's decision for a verdict on his own worth.”

## Scene 3 — The Long Way Up (2:30–3:54)

A rhythmic montage: Malik works at a repair shop, studies business and technology after work, practices useful skills and takes small freelance jobs. He is rejected, makes mistakes and starts again. Tunde reminds him to build small skills instead of waiting for a miracle.

**TUNDE:** “What if you build ten small skills instead?”  
**MALIK:** “I don't even know where to start.”  
**TUNDE:** “Start with the thing in front of you. Finish it. Then learn the next thing.”

Malik learns that online hype is not research. A risky attempt fails; he records what he learned and returns to work. He pays a household bill. Pa quietly gives him an extra portion at dinner.

## Scene 4 — The Noise of the Market (3:54–5:16)

Malik discovers memecoins. Screens fill with memes, loud promises and rapidly changing numbers. He is tempted by the idea that one lucky move could solve everything. He makes mistakes and loses money he should not have risked. He does not borrow money or chase losses; he closes the app and writes down what went wrong.

**TUNDE:** “The loudest person online is not always the one who knows anything.”  
**MALIK:** “I can study it. I can learn the risks.”

Narration: “The market did not care how badly Malik needed a win. Desperation was not a strategy.”

## Scene 5 — The Months That Tested Him (5:16–6:42)

Rain gives way to dry-season light. Malik keeps working, learning and helping at home. He protects family essentials and keeps his main income. Some attempts fail; he accepts uncertainty and becomes more careful. His reliable work grows and he contributes to repairs at Pa's workshop.

**MAMA:** “You look tired.”  
**MALIK:** “I thought success would feel like one big moment. It feels like doing the right small thing again.”  
**MAMA:** “Then keep doing the right small thing. And remember to sleep.”

## Scene 6 — The Turning Point (6:42–8:04)

After months of learning, a project Malik has researched attracts a large community. Its value rises sharply. He checks his records, considers the risks and talks with Tunde. The fictional portfolio valuation climbs to **$100,000,000**. Malik does not celebrate wildly; he steps away from the screen and thinks of his family.

**TUNDE:** “Whatever happens, don't let a number make the decision for you.”  
**MALIK:** “I know. I won't confuse a screen with money in my hand.”

Narration: “After months of failure, study and patience, the impossible number appeared. One hundred million dollars. A number so large it did not feel real.”

**On-screen disclaimer:** “A fictional outcome. Memecoin markets are extremely volatile; losses can be severe.”

## Scene 7 — What Success Is For (8:04–9:36)

Malik returns home. He explains that he has arranged to address urgent bills, repair the workshop and make a careful plan for the family. He does not promise endless luxury. He wants his parents to rest.

**PA:** “Is this real?”  
**MALIK:** “It's real enough to take care of what we need. I want us to make decisions carefully.”  
**MAMA:** “We were proud of you when you came home with empty pockets and still shared what you had.”

Pa embraces Malik. The renovated workshop opens in warm morning light. Pa touches the new tools with the same care he once gave the old ones.

**PA:** “You didn't just change the house, my son. You gave me back my work.”

Narration: “The money changed their circumstances. It did not create the love that had carried them there.”

## Scene 8 — The Last Signal (9:36–10:48)

At sunset, Malik looks across Lagos. A luxury car passes below—the same symbol that once made him feel small. He barely notices. He returns to the family table.

**MALIK (V.O.):** “I thought I was chasing money. I was trying to give the people I love a little peace.”

Fade to black. Title: **THE LAST SIGNAL**.  
Final line: “Your beginning does not have to decide your ending.”

## Visual and audio direction

- **Look:** Stylized cinematic 3D, detailed Lagos-inspired streets, atmospheric haze, expressive character acting, shallow depth of field and deliberate camera moves.
- **Palette:** Cool dawn blues and muted earth tones at the start; restrained warm gold as Malik gains stability; intimate golden light for the family reunion.
- **Music:** Original ambient piano-and-pad score. Keep dialogue clear; use silence at the breakup and the $100M reveal. Add licensed or self-recorded Lagos ambience, workshop sounds, rain, footsteps and door sounds in the final mix.
- **Three.js:** Procedural city environment, buildings, road, low-poly Malik placeholder, lighting and camera movement. Replace placeholder character with custom rigged GLB models for final-quality animation.

## Responsible storytelling

The $100M event is a fictional, exceptional outcome—not a promise or repeatable strategy. The story emphasizes learning, honest work, caution, family responsibility and emotional growth. Malik's value is never dependent on wealth, and the ending is about care rather than revenge or status.
